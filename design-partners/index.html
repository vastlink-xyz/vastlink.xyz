What is a crypto custody policy for RIAs?

In crypto custody for RIAs, a practical policy defines permitted custody arrangements, safeguards, decision rights, transfer procedures, reporting, continuity, and evidence of control effectiveness. It should let a firm answer one question at any time: who approved this transfer, under which policy, based on what evidence, and what would happen if that person could not act tomorrow?

Why this conversation matters in 2026

The SEC proposed a new crypto custody framework on 1st October 2026. It has not become a final rule.

The proposal would provide limited paths for investment advisers and regulated funds to maintain certain crypto assets in self-custody, subject to conditions. In particular, an adviser would need to document, for each relevant asset and at least quarterly, why no qualified custodian is available. The proposed requirements also address safeguarding systems, joint authorization, client reporting, and independent control assessment.

For an advisory firm, that makes the central question bigger than “Which wallet or custodian should we choose?” It becomes “Can we explain, operate, and demonstrate our custody decisions, consistently, even when something goes wrong?”

A custody strategy has three layers

Layer The question it answers What can still go wrong without governance
Custody arrangement Who holds or controls the assets, and under what legal arrangement? Responsibilities or provider dependencies are unclear.
Security technology How are keys protected and transactions authorized? Strong technology is configured around weak human processes.
Policy and evidence Who may act, under what conditions, and how is every exception reviewed? Decisions are hard to reconstruct when clients, auditors, or successors ask.

Good providers may address all three layers. Our premise is that firms should be able to understand, review, and retain their own control decisions, rather than depend on opaque workflows or scattered conversations.

The working crypto custody policy framework

These eight sections form a discussion framework, not a substitute for a firm-approved policy or legal advice.

  1. 01

    Permitted custodians and custody-option reviews

    Document which providers and arrangements are acceptable for each asset, why they were selected, who approved the assessment, and when it must be revisited. Capture the alternatives considered rather than just the final provider name.

  2. 02

    Conditions for adviser self-custody

    If relevant, identify the legal basis, an asset-by-asset qualified-custodian availability assessment, segregation of client assets, designated key holders, and a scheduled review. Treat adviser self-custody as a conditional exception, not a default recommendation.

  3. 03

    Signing authority and separation of duties

    Define who can propose, approve, and execute a transaction; when two-person authorization is required; who acts as a management approver; and how permissions change when staff join, leave, or become unavailable.

  4. 04

    Transfer limits and independent verification

    Set rules for new destination addresses, unusual amounts, emergency transfers, independent confirmation, and escalation. Record how an exception was authorized, not just that a transaction succeeded.

  5. 05

    Client reporting and reconciliation

    Specify the transaction records, address-level evidence, reconciliation checks, statement frequency, exception handling, and responsibilities for explaining discrepancies to clients.

  6. 06

    Continuity, incapacity, and succession

    Identify backup decision-makers, access-recovery boundaries, documentation locations, service-provider dependencies, and a rehearsal process that never exposes live credentials.

  7. 07

    Independent assurance and evidence readiness

    Organize approvals, policy versions, exceptions, technical logs, and reconciliations so a qualified independent accountant and the firm's advisers can review the controls. A policy draft does not replace an independent control report.

  8. 08

    Annual review, incidents, and corrective action

    Assign owners for periodic reassessment of providers, key management, cyber risks, authorizations, and operational incidents. Track what changed, why, who approved it, and whether the fix worked.

Frequently asked questions

Has the SEC approved adviser self-custody of client crypto assets?

Not through the October 2026 proposal. The SEC published proposed changes on 1st October 2026, but they are not a final rule. The contemplated self-custody path is limited and conditional, including a documented determination that a qualified custodian is unavailable. Firms should rely on current requirements and qualified legal advice when making custody decisions today.

What belongs in an RIA crypto custody policy?

A useful policy should connect custody choices to accountable people and reviewable evidence. Typical sections cover approved custodians, conditions for self-custody, signing authority, transfer verification, reconciliation and client reporting, incident response, continuity, and independent-control review. The right detail depends on the firm's assets, custody arrangement, risk profile, and legal obligations (see the working framework).

Does a design partner have to custody crypto today?

No. We are interested in firms that are considering client digital-asset exposure as well as firms with existing workflows. Questions about who would hold assets, approve transfers, or document controls can be explored without a live wallet. The program is about clarifying requirements before making a technology commitment.

Is Vastlink a custodian? Would it hold our clients’ assets?

No. Vastlink does not take custody of client assets and is not a qualified custodian. We are exploring the control and evidence layer that would sit on top of whichever custody route a firm chooses. Today Vastlink is at the design and validation stage, so there is no production product holding anyone’s assets.

Is the program free? Is there a product to buy?

The six-week design-partner program has no fee, and there is no production Vastlink custody product for sale through this program. We are asking for your experience and feedback, not a purchase order. Any future commercial proposal would be a separate discussion with terms shared explicitly rather than assumed at the outset.

No. The proposed collaboration uses policy documents, interviews, and simulated scenarios. We will not ask for seed phrases, private keys, production credentials, or custody of your clients' assets. No. Vastlink is exploring a governance workflow and may prepare a working policy draft for discussion.
